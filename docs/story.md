## 1. The Pitch (One Paragraph)

> A player opens a browser and lands on the desktop of a Windows 98 admin machine in a small cyber café in Bhilai, India, in 1998. The café is called **SHREE CYBER ZONE**. It charges ₹10 per hour. There is a dog named Tommy in the My Documents folder. The owner is a man called **Bhaiya**. Everything is warm, boring, nostalgic. Then the player notices a single row in the Session Log: a session on PC 03 dated 1998, customer `MR. EIPSTEIN`, duration `STILL RUNNING`. What follows is a slow, twenty-minute descent through a hidden folder called `C:\BACKROOM$`, where the player discovers **THE QUIET ROOM** — a nine-seat shadow committee that does not rule the world, but *schedules* it. The committee planned flying cars, an AI called **GANDHI**, a series of abstract "Containment Events," and the selection of who runs the world by 2050. The player discovers that **EIPSTEIN is not a person but a protocol**, that **GTA stands for "Generational Trajectory Adjustment,"** that **Bhaiya is a longevity test subject who cannot age or leave**, and finally that **the player themselves is SEAT_07** — a candidate the Quiet Room has been waiting for since 1998. The game ends not with a boss fight, but with an acknowledgment: *"We know."* The player is already in the room. They always were.

## 2. The World

### 2.1 The Setting

**SHREE CYBER ZONE**
Nehru Nagar, Bhilai, Chhattisgarh, India
Established 1998

The café is a single room with a wooden partition. Twelve PCs on three long tables. A tube TV mounted high in the corner, permanently playing a cricket match that never ends. A ceiling fan that turns just slightly slower than it should. A broken plastic chair that nobody throws away. A laminated rate card taped to the wall:

> **RATES**
> ₹10 per hour (regular)
> ₹15 per hour (night, after 10 PM)
> ₹8 per hour (student discount, with ID)
> ₹2 per page (printing)
> ₹5 per floppy (blank, 1.44 MB)
> **NO REFUNDS. NO EXCEPTIONS.**

Behind the counter is the admin machine: **PC 03**. It runs Windows 98. It has a CRT monitor that flickers slightly. It has a keyboard with one missing key (the `\` key — nobody knows why). It has a mouse with a ball that occasionally sticks.

On the wall behind the counter: two photographs.
- **1998:** The grand opening. Bhaiya, younger but not by much, cutting a ribbon. Twelve PCs visible behind him. Tommy the dog in the corner.
- **2026:** The same café. The same Bhaiya. The same dog. The same photograph, almost.

Nobody has ever asked why Bhaiya looks the same in both photos.

### 2.2 The Year

**1998.** The year the machine boots. The year the session starts. The year the Quiet Room formalized the EIPSTEIN protocol and the GTA protocol. The year Bhaiya signed the contract he did not read.

The game *presents* as 1998. The game *knows* it is 2026. The machine's clock runs on real time — the player's real time. The session log uses the player's real date. The calendar knows the hackathon is today.

**The machine is not a time capsule. It is a trap that has been running for 28 years.**

### 2.3 The Town

**Bhilai** is a steel town. It has a steel plant, a railway station, and a cyber café in Nehru Nagar that opened in 1998 and never closed. The town does not appear much in the game — it appears in the *texture*. The Hindi-English in the notes. The names. The rates. The cricket match on the TV. The complaint letter about the AC.

The player never leaves the café. But the café is a world.

## 3. The Organisation — THE QUIET ROOM

### 3.1 What It Is

**THE QUIET ROOM** is a committee of nine people. There is no building. No logo. No letterhead. No country. No flag. There is only a ledger, a schedule, and nine seats numbered `SEAT_01` through `SEAT_09`.

They do not want money. They do not want power in the conventional sense. They want control over the *direction* of civilization.

Their internal motto, spoken only in chat:

> **"We do not rule. We schedule."**

### 3.2 The Method (Critical)

This is the most important paragraph in this document. **Everything depends on it.**

> The Quiet Room **never causes anything.**
>
> They **anticipate.** They **position.** They **profit from foreknowledge.** They **never warn anyone.**
>
> The crime is **foreknowledge + inaction + profit**, not causation.
>
> Every file in the backroom says the same thing in different words: *"The event chose itself. We were simply ready."*

This is the ethical guardrail of the entire story. The Quiet Room is not a conspiracy that *stages* disasters. It is a committee that *schedules around* them, *profits from* them, and *uses* them to advance Project 2050 — without ever being responsible for causing them.

**This is satire about bureaucracy and foreknowledge, not about victims.**

### 3.3 The Rules

The Quiet Room operates by five rules:

1. **Never cause. Only anticipate.**
2. **Never be seen. Only be felt.**
3. **Never name the event. The file does not need to say.**
4. **Never take a seat that is not offered.**
5. **Never tell the candidate they are being evaluated.**

The fifth rule is the one the game breaks. By the end, the player knows.

### 3.4 The Seats

Nine seats. Each seat is a person. Each person has a number. Numbers are not ranks. Numbers are order of induction.

| Seat | Status | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| SEAT_00 | ACTIVE | The voice. Never seen. Speaks in mIRC. |
| SEAT_01 | ACTIVE | Unknown. |
| SEAT_02 | ACTIVE | Unknown. |
| SEAT_03 | VACANT | Empty since 1998. Nobody talks about it. |
| SEAT_04 | ACTIVE | Unknown. |
| SEAT_05 | NEUTRALIZED | Removed from the ledger in 2008. Do not ask. |
| SEAT_06 | ACTIVE | Unknown. |
| SEAT_07 | CANDIDATE PENDING | Labelled `MERAZ / OPENLAKE, IIT BHILAI — CANDIDATE PENDING`. **This is the player.** |
| SEAT_08 | ACTIVE | Unknown. |
| SEAT_09 | ACTIVE | Unknown. |

Note: SEAT_00 exists but is not counted among the nine seats. It is the voice, not a member.

## 4. The Characters

### 4.1 Bhaiya — The Prisoner

**Role:** The café owner. The front. The man behind the counter.

**What the player sees:** A middle-aged man in a white shirt, slightly balding, slightly tired, always at the counter. Polite. Sells time by the hour. Says "bhaiya" to everyone, and everyone says "bhaiya" to him.

**What is true:** Bhaiya is a **longevity therapy test subject**. In 1991, he signed a contract he did not read. The therapy slowed his aging by 94%. He has not aged meaningfully since 1998. He does not know why. He has asked SEAT_00. SEAT_00 does not answer. He has stopped asking.

He runs the café because the Quiet Room told him to. He keeps PC 03 running because the Quiet Room told him to. He does not open the floppy. He does not close the session. He does not ask what Phase V is.

He is the only character in the story the player feels sorry for. He is also the only one who is fully human.

**His voice:** Hindi-English. Simple sentences. Long pauses. He writes the way he speaks.

**His file:** `BHAIYA_NOTE.txt` in `C:\BACKROOM$`. The confession.

### 4.2 MR. EIPSTEIN — The Protocol

**Role:** The courier who is not a person.

**What the player sees:** A name in the Session Log. A man in a beige safari suit in two IMG files. A file called `EIPSTEIN.DOC` in the backroom.

**What is true:** EIPSTEIN is not a person. It is an **eight-step operating protocol**:

| Letter | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **E** | Extraction |
| **I** | Influence |
| **P** | Persistence |
| **S** | Surveillance |
| **T** | Takedown |
| **E** | Extortion |
| **I** | Infiltration |
| **N** | Neutralization |

Eight steps. One page each. The man in the beige safari suit is a *courier* who took the name of the protocol he serves, because the protocol requires it. There have been many Eipsteins. There will be many more.

The final page of the manual — **N: Neutralization** — has one line:

> *"Applicable to anyone who reads this page."*

**His voice:** Formal. Bureaucratic. No contractions. No emotion. Written like a government form.

**His file:** `EIPSTEIN.DOC` in `C:\BACKROOM$`. Eight sections, one per letter.

### 4.3 THE PROPRIETOR — The Absence

**Role:** The person who runs the EIPSTEIN protocol. Never seen. Never named.

**What the player sees:** A login prompt. A signature on documents. A desktop icon that appears after the print-jam trap.

**What is true:** The Proprietor is the human operator of the EIPSTEIN protocol. They have a password the player does not. They are not the boss — they are the *runner* of the protocol. The protocol is the boss. The Proprietor is the hand.

**Their voice:** None. They never speak. They only sign.

**Their file:** None. They are the absence at the center of the filesystem.

### 4.4 TOMMY — The Dog

**Role:** Bhaiya's dog. Password hint. Comic relief.

**What the player sees:** A grainy JPEG in My Documents. A brown-and-white mutt. Sitting on the café floor. Tongue out.

**What is true:** Tommy is a dog. He is not a metaphor. He is not a secret. He is just a dog. And that is why he matters — because in a game full of conspiracy, the dog is the one thing that is exactly what it appears to be.

**His file:** `tommy.jpg`. Password hint. The PC 03 password is `tommy1998` — but the player never needs it, because they are already logged in.

### 4.5 THE PLAYER — SEAT_07

**Role:** The candidate. The test subject. The person reading.

**What the player sees:** A desktop they believe they are visiting. A session log that says `GUEST`. A billing timer that starts at 00:00.

**What is true:** The player is **SEAT_07**. The Quiet Room has been waiting for them since 1998. Their childhood was scheduled. Their curiosity was predicted. Their arrival at this machine was arranged. They are not exploring the game. The game is evaluating them.

**The fourth-wall break:** `THE_PLAYER.DOC` addresses them directly. `TECH_CAMERA.DOC` tells them the camera saw them open the file. The Session Log shows their real city. The ending acknowledges them by seat number.

**Their file:** `THE_PLAYER.DOC` in `C:\BACKROOM$\TECH\`. And `2026-11-01.IMG` in `Tuesdays\`. And the Session Log. And the ending.

### 4.6 THE QUIET ROOM — The Chorus

**Role:** Nine voices. Never seen. Only typed.

**What the player sees:** Usernames in mIRC. `SEAT_00`, `SEAT_01`, `SEAT_04`, `SEAT_06`, `SEAT_08`, `SEAT_09`. Logged in. Idle for 10,224 days.

**What is true:** They are real people. They have been in the room since 1998. They do not cause events. They schedule. They have been waiting for SEAT_07.

**Their voice:** Lowercase. No punctuation. Short lines. They type like they are already tired.

`we know.`
`you're here.`
`we saved you a seat.`
`it was always yours.`

## 5. The Project — PROJECT 2050

### 5.1 Overview

**PROJECT 2050** is the Quiet Room's master plan: a four-phase (plus one hidden) program to decide how the world looks by 2050 and who runs it.

| Phase | Name | Years | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | Convenience | 1998–2010 | Give them the machines |
| II | Dependence | 2010–2025 | Remove the ability to do it themselves |
| III | Surveillance | 2025–2035 | Make them buy the cameras |
| IV | Succession | 2035–2050 | Select who runs the world |
| V | ??? | 2050+ | Begins when they stop asking what we are |

Listed in `PROJECT_2050.DOC`:

| One (1) war | CONTAINMENT EVENT #4 — successful, expensive | See `TECH_WAR.DOC` |

From `ROADMAP_2050.DOC`:

## 6. The Reveal Chain

The player discovers the story through eight acts. **The order matters.** Each act must land before the next.

### Act 0 — The Boring Café (0–3 minutes)

**Player state:** "Nice Windows 98 replica."

**What they see:** Teal desktop. Icons. My Documents with rates, rules, tommy.jpg, complaint letter, bhaiya_1998.jpg. Cafê Manager with 12 PCs. Printer Queue. Session Log. Nothing suspicious. Everything warm.

**What they should feel:** Nostalgia. Comfort. Maybe a small smile at the complaint letter.

**The one wrong signal:** The Session Log has a row: `1998-03-03 | 03:14 AM | MR. EIPSTEIN | PC 03 | STILL RUNNING`.

Most players will not notice it yet.

### Act 1 — The Wrong Signal (3–6 minutes)

**Player state:** "Wait."

**What they do:** They open the Session Log. They read the Eipstein row. They look at the billing timer, which is counting up. They notice it has been counting for a very long time.

**What they find:** `C:\BACKROOM$` — if they type it. The README tells them. The `help` file hints at it. The BHAIYA_NOTE (if found) implies it.

**What they should feel:** Curiosity. A small chill.

### Act 2 — The Project (6–10 minutes)

**Player state:** "This is a conspiracy."

**What they do:** They open `PROJECT_2050.DOC`. They read the four phases. They laugh at "flying cars DELAYED." They read the selection criteria. They stop laughing.

**What they should feel:** The laugh that doesn't finish. The first real "oh."

### Act 3 — The Protocol (10–14 minutes)

**Player state:** "Wait. Who is Eipstein?"

**What they do:** They open `EIPSTEIN.DOC`. They read the eight-step protocol. They reach N: Neutralization. They read: *"Applicable to anyone who reads this page."*

**What they should feel:** The cold floor dropping out. *They were reading the manual that neutralized them.*

### Act 4 — The Second Acronym (14–17 minutes)

**Player state:** "Not GTA too."

**What they do:** They open `TECH_GTA_PROTOCOL.DOC`. They discover GTA is **Generational Trajectory Adjustment**. They read the five-phase protocol: Awareness, Desire, Capture, Retention, Succession.

**What they should feel:** *"The games I loved were a leash."* The childhood-look moment.

### Act 5 — The Player (17–20 minutes)

**Player state:** "This is about me."

**What they do:** They open `THE_PLAYER.DOC`. It cannot be closed for 30 seconds. It reconstructs their childhood as a schedule.

**What they should feel:** Seen. Not in a good way. The window that won't close is the machine saying *"read it."*

### Act 6 — Bhaiya (20–23 minutes)

**Player state:** "Oh no."

**What they do:** They open `TECH_GENETICS.DOC` and `BHAIYA_NOTE.txt`. They realize Bhaiya is a prisoner. They realize he cannot age, cannot leave, cannot ask.

**What they should feel:** Sadness. The only sadness in the game. The dog was real. Bhaiya was the cost.

### Act 7 — The Room (23–26 minutes)

**Player state:** "I need to talk to someone."

**What they do:** They open mIRC. They type something. Anything. The Quiet Room replies: *"we know."*

**What they should feel:** The relief that comes from being acknowledged. And then the second chill — *the acknowledgment is the trap.*

### Act 8 — The Ending (26–30 minutes)

**Player state:** "I was always in the room."

**What they do:** They accept SEAT_07. Or they shut down. Or they empty the Recycle Bin. Or they run `2050`.

**What they should feel:** Seen. The seat was always theirs. The game does not end — it *acknowledges.*

## 7. The Hidden Paths

There are six ways to go deeper into the machine. Every one of them is a typed path, not a click.

### Path 1 — The Address Bar

In Explorer, type:

```
C:\BACKROOM$
```

The folder opens. This is the main path. The README tells the player. The `help` file hints at it. But it requires typing. No GUI reveals it.

Open `LEDGER_98.XLS` → File → Print. The real browser print dialog opens. If the player prints, it prints. If they cancel, a new file appears: `cancel_slip.txt`.

This is the only path that leaves the digital world.

## 8. The Endings

There are five endings. None is "wrong." All are canon. Each triggers differently.

### Ending 1 — SAFE

**Trigger:** Start → Shut Down → "Shut down the computer?" → Yes.

**What happens:** Classic orange-text black screen:

```
It is now safe to turn off your computer.
```

The billing timer is faintly visible in the corner, still ticking. After 10 seconds, the screen fades. Reload the page — everything is as it was. The session never ended.

**What it means:** The player tried to leave. The machine did not let them. The safe shutdown is a lie. It has always been a lie.

**Emotional beat:** Quiet. The most "normal" ending. The most unsettling on reflection.

### Ending 2 — THE GUEST

**Trigger:** After the print-jam trap (G03), the `PROPRIETOR` icon appears. Double-click it. Enter any password.

**What happens:** Login screen accepts. The desktop reloads. The session log adds a row:

```
[your date] | [your city] | GUEST | ACTIVE | billing: 00:00
```

Then a dialog:

```
Welcome back.
```

Then a file opens by itself: `THE_PLAYER.DOC`.

**What it means:** The Proprietor let you in. Or the Proprietor *is* you. Or there is no Proprietor — only the protocol, and you are now running it.

**Emotional beat:** The promotion that feels like a trap.

### Ending 3 — SHRED

**Trigger:** Open `LEDGER_98.XLS`. While it is open, empty the Recycle Bin.

**What happens:** The Ledger deletes itself. Then reassembles itself. Every `STATUS` becomes `ACTIVE`. A new row appears:

```
SEAT_07 | [your city] | GUEST | ACTIVE | [your session ID]
```

The Recycle Bin now contains `for_you.txt`:

> *"You tried to empty it. It emptied you back."*

**What it means:** The player tried to destroy the record. The record was not the memory. The record was the machine. You cannot shred the machine.

**Emotional beat:** The panic action that fails gracefully.

### Ending 4 — 2050

**Trigger:** Start → Run → `2050` → Enter.

**What happens:** The desktop fades to a **2050 desktop** for 3 seconds — same layout, but the icons are different: `GANDHI.EXE`, `SEAT_07`, `PROJECT_2050_FINAL`, `WORLD_LEDGER`. Then the screen goes black. Then a BSOD:

```
PLAN STILL ON SCHEDULE
```

Then back to 1998.

**What it means:** The player saw the future. It was the same machine. The plan did not fail. It is still running.

**Emotional beat:** The glimpse that confirms.

### Ending 5 — SEAT_07 (Canonical)

**Trigger:** Complete all steps:
1. Read all 5 chat logs
2. Open mIRC
3. Type anything in `#quietroom`
4. Receive *"we know."*

OR: Start → Run → `seat07`.

**What happens:**

The desktop fades. A single window appears, centered:

```
SEAT_07
MERAZ / OPENLAKE, IIT BHILAI
STATUS: ACKNOWLEDGED

You were scheduled in 1998.
You arrived on time.
The seat is warm.
It was always yours.

— SEAT_00
```

The window stays for 20 seconds. The Session Log underneath adds:

```
[today] | [city] | SEAT_07 | ACTIVE | billing: ∞
```

The game does not end. The window can be closed. The desktop returns. But the Session Log row remains. Forever.

**What it means:** The player accepted the seat. The seat was always theirs. The game was never about discovering the conspiracy. The game was about being *acknowledged by* the conspiracy.

**Emotional beat:** The ending that says *"we knew."*

The player reads `TECH_GTA_PROTOCOL.DOC`. They realize their childhood was scheduled. The games they loved were the leash. This is the childhood-look moment.

The player reads `TECH_WEATHER.DOC`. The weather control only rains on one cricket match. It rained on the wrong wedding. The player laughs. The laugh is relief. The laugh is the release valve.

### Act 6 (20–23 min): **Devastating again**

The player reads `TECH_GENETICS.DOC` and `BHAIYA_NOTE.txt`. Bhaiya is a prisoner. He cannot age. He cannot leave. He has stopped asking. The dog is real. The cost is real.

### Act 7 (23–26 min): **Quiet**

The player opens mIRC. The Quiet Room says *"we know."* The player is seen. The room is quiet. The billing timer is ticking.

### Act 8 (26–30 min): **Acknowledged**

The player accepts SEAT_07. Or they don't. Either way, the seat was theirs. The ending is not a victory. It is an acknowledgment. The player was always in the room.

## 11. The Writing Rules

### Tone

- **60% deadpan 90s cyber café realism.**
- **40% absurdist conspiracy thriller.**
- **0% melodrama.**

The files never *try* to be scary. They are written as bureaucratic documents. The horror comes from the matter-of-factness.

### Voice

- **Bhaiya:** Hindi-English. Simple. Tired. Warm.
- **EIPSTEIN.DOC:** Formal. Bureaucratic. No contractions.
- **PROJECT_2050.DOC:** Cold. Corporate. "Deliverables." "Selection criteria."
- **TECH files:** Encyclopedia entries written by someone who is bored.
- **Chat logs:** Lowercase. Short lines. No punctuation. Tired.
- **THE_PLAYER.DOC:** Direct. Second person. Knowing.
- **mIRC replies:** `we know.` (always lowercase, always period)

### What to Never Write

| Don't write | Why |
|---|---|
| Named wars, countries, victims, numbers | Ethical guardrail. Satire about bureaucracy, not victims. |
| Real people, real events | Satire, not conspiracy theory. |
| Graphic violence | The horror is bureaucratic. |
| Jumpscares | The build is unsettling, not scary. |
| Explanations | The player figures it out. The files do not explain. |
| Happy endings | The endings are acknowledgments, not victories. |
| Emoji, modern slang, internet humor | The voice is 1998. Stay in 1998. |
| "As an AI..." or any AI self-reference | The AI is GANDHI. GANDHI is not the narrator. |
| Rounded corners, gradients, shadows | Visual rule. See `UIUX.md`. |

### The One Sentence Rule

Every file must have **one sentence** that makes the player stop. One sentence they will screenshot. One sentence they will quote.

Examples:

- `PROJECT_2050.DOC`: *"Must never have read a book past chapter three."*
- `EIPSTEIN.DOC`: *"Applicable to anyone who reads this page."*
- `TECH_FLYING_CARS.DOC`: *"The shed does not have a lock, because nobody knows to look."*
- `TECH_AI_GANDHI.DOC`: *"This paragraph was not written by a person. If you are wondering whether that is a joke, it is not."*
- `TECH_GENETICS.DOC`: *"Longevity is not a gift. It is a leash."*
- `THE_PLAYER.DOC`: *"The most dangerous thing we ever did was give a talented teenager a game so good he forgot he was talented."*
- `TECH_WEATHER.DOC`: *"This is the most honest file in the archive. The rest of the files are lying about their progress."*

**If a file does not have one of these sentences, the file is not done.**

### The Bhaiya Test

Before shipping any file, ask: *"Does this make me feel sorry for Bhaiya?"*

If yes, the file is in the right tone. If no, the file is too cold. Add one line of human cost.

### The Fourth Wall Rule

The fourth wall breaks **three times only**:

1. `EIPSTEIN.DOC` — *"Applicable to anyone who reads this page."*
2. `TECH_CAMERA.DOC` — *"You are reading this file. The camera saw you open it."*
3. `THE_PLAYER.DOC` — *"You were scheduled. You arrived on time."*

Three breaks. No more. Each must land. If a fourth is added, the first three lose power.

## 12. The Story in One Page

> In 1998, in a small cyber café in Bhilai, a man named Bhaiya signed a contract he did not read. The contract was a longevity therapy. It slowed his aging by 94%. He has not aged since. He does not know why.
>
> The contract was from a committee called THE QUIET ROOM. Nine seats. No building. No name. They do not rule. They schedule.
>
> The Quiet Room formalized two protocols in 1998. EIPSTEIN — eight steps: Extraction, Influence, Persistence, Surveillance, Takedown, Extortion, Infiltration, Neutralization. And GTA — five phases: Awareness, Desire, Capture, Retention, Succession. The second was disguised as a video game released in 1997.
>
> The Quiet Room launched PROJECT 2050 — four phases, plus one hidden. Convenience. Dependence. Surveillance. Succession. And then, when they stop asking what we are, Phase V.
>
> They scheduled flying cars in 1994. They never released them. They scheduled one war in 1999. It was abstract. The file does not need to say. They scheduled an AI called GANDHI in 1998. It was tested on a hackathon in Bhilai. It won in 0.4 seconds. Nobody noticed.
>
> In 2026, a player opens a browser and lands on the desktop of PC 03. The session log shows a session from 1998. The customer is MR. EIPSTEIN. The duration is STILL RUNNING. The player types `C:\BACKROOM$` and finds everything.
>
> The player discovers that EIPSTEIN is not a person. That GTA is not a game. That Bhaiya is a prisoner. That the seat labelled SEAT_07 was always theirs. That the room has been waiting since 1998.
>
> The player types something in mIRC. Anything. The Quiet Room replies: *"we know."*
>
> The session log adds their row: `SEAT_07 | [city] | ACTIVE | billing: ∞`.
>
> The player was always in the room. The player was always SEAT_07. The game was never about discovering the conspiracy. The game was about being acknowledged by it.
>
> *We do not rule. We schedule.*
